# Coaching Hockey in Cebu: 5 Lessons That Changed How I Lead

*By Arnel – Coach & RinkStop Coordination Lead*

*May 8, 2026*

I remember the first time I blew a whistle in Cebu. It was 2019. I had just moved from Chicago, hockey sticks in one hand and a dream in the other. The kids looked at me like I was from another planet. "Coach, what is this game?" they asked. I smiled and said, "Trust me — you're going to love it."

Four years later, I'm still here. And those kids? They're skating, competing, and dreaming bigger than I ever imagined. But hockey wasn't the only thing that grew. I grew right along with it. Here are five lessons coaching in the Philippines taught me about leadership, community, and staying true to your mission.

## 1. Start Before You're Ready

When I first started CoachBoard — our digital drill-drawing tool — I didn't have all the features polished. I didn't have a fancy office or a team of developers. I had a vision and a laptop. I remember sketching drill diagrams on napkins at a local café in Cebu, thinking, "If just one coach finds this useful, it's worth it." That's how RinkStop's tools were born. Imperfect, but real. Done beats perfect every single time.

## 2. Community Over Competition

Back in Chicago, hockey was about winning. Here in Cebu, it's about showing up. When our youth league started, we had 12 kids. Now we have over 200. But the growth wasn't from flashy marketing — it was from parents telling parents. One family would bring their cousin, and that cousin would bring a neighbor. I learned that when you build something genuine, people don't just join — they carry it forward.

## 3. Tools Change the Game

Our Scoresheet app — the digital scorekeeping tool with PDF exports, time tracking, and penalty logging — was born from pure frustration. I was tired of losing paper scoresheets in the humidity. I remember one tournament where we lost three games' worth of stats because the paper literally disintegrated. Going digital wasn't a luxury. It was survival. Now coaches across Southeast Asia use it. Sometimes the simplest problems lead to the most powerful solutions.

## 4. Culture Eats Strategy

I brought Chicago-style hockey culture to a country where field hockey was more common than ice hockey. That's a recipe for disaster — unless you adapt. I had to learn that building a hockey culture here meant respecting the local context. We mixed drills with Filipino values: bayanihan (community unity), pakikisama (getting along), and diskarte (resourcefulness). Hockey became ours, not just imported from America.

## 5. Your Weird Background Is Your Superpower

I'm a Filipino-American from Chicago. I coached in Africa before settling in Cebu. My stepdad traded corn options on the CBOT floor. None of that screams "hockey coach." But it's exactly why RinkStop works. We see the game differently. We build differently. We coach differently. The things that make you feel out of place are often the things that make you irreplaceable.

---

Looking at where RinkStop is today — a global hockey directory, digital tools for coaches, a growing community across three continents — I'm reminded that none of this was in the original plan. But every detour taught me something. Every awkward moment on the ice built trust. Every kid who fell and got back up reminded me why this matters.

If you're building something and feel like an outsider, stay. Your perspective is exactly what the world needs.
