# Why Youth Hockey's Biggest Growth Isn't in the NHL — It's Overseas

**By Arnel Larracas | RinkStop.com**

I've coached hockey in Chicago and Cebu, Philippines. I've seen what the stats confirm: the sport's biggest growth story isn't Connor McDavid breaking speed records at 24.61 mph. It's a 12-year-old kid in Southeast Asia picking up a stick for the first time through an NHL Street Hockey program.

**The Numbers Are Real**

USA Hockey membership has surged past 560,000 registered youth players. The NHL/NHLPA Learn to Play and First Shift programs introduced hockey to over 43,000 boys and girls this season alone. Girls' participation jumped 26% in the U.S. and hit record-breaking numbers in Canada, where nearly half of all First Shift participants are female.

But here's what most people miss: the global pipeline.

NHL Street Hockey now operates in seven countries: Finland, Sweden, Czechia, Australia, UK, China, and Germany. The program is school-based, reaching an estimated 30,000 student participants who'd never see an NHL game live. The International Ice Hockey Federation projects that Southeast Asia alone could add 50,000+ youth players by 2030.

**Why This Matters for Hockey's Future**

More players means more fans. More fans means more revenue. More revenue means more rinks, more equipment demand, more coaching development. It's a flywheel, and it's already spinning.

The GWEC's decision to expand the Asia-Pacific Wind Energy Summit isn't hockey-related — but the infrastructure investments coming to countries like the Philippines (new multi-sheet facilities, equipment manufacturing, technician training programs) create secondary ecosystems that hockey can plug into.

I saw it firsthand in Cebu. When we started coaching clinics, we had 15 kids and no real ice. Today we have structured programs, real equipment from NHLPA Goals & Dreams donations, and kids asking about NCAA Division I scholarships.

**The Investment Angle**

150+ new ice facilities opened in the U.S. between 2015-2023, concentrated in Sun Belt states. The infrastructure spending is following the players. But the next wave of growth isn't in Arizona or Texas — it's in countries where hockey barely existed five years ago.

The question for the global hockey industry: are you building rinks and programs in traditional markets, or planting seeds where the next billion fans are?

I know where I'm planting mine.
