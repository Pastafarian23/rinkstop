Something interesting is happening in global hockey that most sports industry people haven't caught onto yet.

The NHL/NHLPA just reported that Learn to Play and First Shift programs introduced hockey to over 43,000 kids this season. Girls' participation in the US jumped 26% in a single year. And for the first time, NHL Street Hockey is operating in 7 countries beyond North America.

But here's what really caught my attention: the infrastructure side. Over 150 new ice facilities opened in the US between 2015-2023, mostly in non-traditional markets like Texas, Florida, and Arizona. The NHL salary cap hitting $104M for 2026-27 means more investment in ancillary industries — facilities, equipment, coaching, tech.

As someone who coaches hockey in Cebu, Philippines, I've seen firsthand what this global expansion looks like at the grassroots level. It's not just about building rinks — it's about building ecosystems. Coaching programs, equipment supply chains, player development pathways.

RinkStop was built to be the connective tissue for this expanding global hockey community. A directory that helps you find teams, players, leagues, and facilities — anywhere in the world.

The sport is growing. The question is whether you're positioned to capture that growth.
