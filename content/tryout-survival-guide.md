**The Hockey Tryout Survival Guide**
*What every parent needs to know before, during, and after tryouts*
By Coach Arnel Larracas · RinkStop.com · 2025-26 Edition

### Subtitle / Hook
Every year, hundreds of thousands of kids show up to hockey tryouts across North America. Most parents have no idea what's about to happen, what coaches are actually evaluating, or how to support their kid through a process that can feel brutal even when it goes well.

This guide is for you. It comes from 20 years of coaching — at every level from mites to juniors, in three countries, on rinks from Chicago to the Philippines. It's the guide I wish every hockey parent had before their kid's first tryout.

### 1.2 What Coaches Are Actually Evaluating
- It's not just skill. Coaches look at 5 things, in this order:
  1. **Skating** — speed, edges, balance, transition. You can't teach this in 2 weeks.
  2. **Hockey sense** — where to be, when to be there. Some kids have it, others don't.
  3. **Work ethic** — does the kid keep going when tired? When losing?
  4. **Coachability** — does the kid listen, adjust, take feedback?
  5. **Skill with the puck** — last, not first. Most coaches can teach skills if the other four are there.
- Why coaches rank these in this order (and why parents usually have it backwards)
- **[A: A real tryout story that shows all 5 in action]**

### 1.3 The 2-Week Prep Plan
What you can realistically change in 14 days. What you can't.
- Skating is the only skill you can meaningfully improve in 2 weeks. Edges, crossovers, transitions.
- Equipment fit matters more than you think. Skates too big? Knee pads too low? They'll notice.
- Rest > overtraining. A kid who rested will outperform a kid who was "crushing it" all summer.

## Part 2: The Tryout Day (6 pages)

### 2.1 The Drop-Off
- Arrive 45-60 min early. Not 15. Not 30.
- What to expect in the lobby: chaos, kids in half-tied gear, parents who didn't read this guide
- Where to stand, where NOT to stand (the bench area, behind the glass, near the bench door)
- Why you shouldn't watch from the stands (and when you should)

### 2.2 What Actually Happens at a Tryout
- 60-90 min typical. Warm-up, drills, scrimmage, sometimes a skills test.
- The first 5 min set the tone. Coaches form strong first impressions.
- The scrimmage is where 80% of decisions are made. Drills are data points, scrimmages are verdicts.
- Why the kids who stand out aren't always the most skilled
- **[A: A breakdown of a real tryout you ran. "Here's what I was looking for in each drill."]**

### 2.3 The 3 Things That Disqualify Kids on the Spot
1. **Not listening.** If a coach gives instruction and the kid is still messing around, that's a 1-way ticket off the team.
2. **Skating through drills.** If a kid coasts, take shortcuts, doesn't finish, they're done. Effort > outcome.
3. **Bad attitude with refs or teammates.** 1 incident isn't fatal. 3+ is.
- **[A: The worst tryout moment you ever witnessed. What happened, what the parent did, what the kid did.]**

### 2.4 What If My Kid Has a Bad Tryout?
- It happens. Even good kids have bad days. Stomach bug, didn't sleep, intimidated.
- Most coaches know this. They look for the trend across multiple tryouts, not one bad day.
- What to say (and what NOT to say) in the car afterward
- The single biggest mistake parents make: debriefing the entire ride home

## Part 3: After Tryouts — The Hardest Part (5 pages)

### 3.1 The Wait
- Most associations post results within 24-72 hours. Some take a week.
- Don't call the coach asking when results will be posted. They'll post when they post.
- The phone should stay in your kid's pocket during the wait, not yours

### 3.2 If They Made It
- Celebrate briefly, then reset. The team is the goal, the season is the work.
- Get the schedule. Get the gear list. Get the parent volunteer signup. Be a good teammate-parent.
- The "no whining on the bench" rule (or: why the best parents stay quiet)

### 3.3 If They Didn't Make It
- This is the chapter I wish every parent would read twice.
- Don't blame the coach. Don't blame the kid. Don't blame the politics (even if it's true).
- The 3 conversations to have:
  1. "I'm proud of you for trying."
  2. "What did you learn about your game?"
  3. "What do you want to do next?"
- Why cutting isn't the worst thing that can happen. Quitting is.
- When to consider switching associations, when to consider a different sport
- The "growth mindset" trap — don't be the parent who turns every cut into a TED talk
- **[A: The story of a kid you coached who got cut and what happened next. Real outcome, not a fairy tale.]**

### 3.4 If They Got Cut But Want to Try Again
- The honest conversation about why
- 6-month plan: what to work on, what associations to consider, what to do this summer
- The "right" age to push vs. the "right" age to step back

### 3.5 The Parent's Job vs. The Kid's Job
- Your job: logistics, emotional support, transportation, the silent cheerleader
- Their job: effort, attitude, work between practices
- Where most parents cross the line, and how to course-correct

## Part 5: Checklists (2 pages — printable)

### 5.1 Day-Of Tryout Checklist
- [ ] Skates sharpened (3 days before, not the night before)
- [ ] Stick(s) — at least 2, pre-taped
- [ ] Helmet, gloves, shoulder pads, elbow pads, pants, shin guards
- [ ] Jersey, socks (matching the team color if known)
- [ ] Water bottle (labeled)
- [ ] Snack for after (real food, not a candy bar)
- [ ] Towel, change of clothes for the ride home
- [ ] Arrive 45-60 min early
- [ ] Phone in the bag, not in your hand

### 5.2 The 2-Week Prep Checklist
- [ ] Edge work: 10 min/day, 4 days/week (find an open skate)
- [ ] Crossovers both directions, both feet
- [ ] Transitions (front-to-back, back-to-front) on both sides
- [ ] Equipment check: skates fitted, helmet CSA-certified, mouthguard, jock/jill
- [ ] Sleep: 9+ hours/night for 2 weeks
- [ ] Hydration: half body weight in oz of water per day
- [ ] No new foods, no late nights, no big changes

### 5.3 The Car Ride Home — 5 Scripts
- "I loved watching you play."
- "I saw you work really hard on [specific thing]."
- "You didn't get the result you wanted, but you showed up. That's the hard part."
- "What was the best part of the tryout?"
- "What do you want to do next?"
