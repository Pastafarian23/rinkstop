# Commercial Real Estate Outlook: Cook County 2024‑2025

*Published: Draft*

## Executive Summary
Commercial real estate (CRE) in Cook County is undergoing a modest re‑balance as office demand softens while industrial and mixed‑use assets gain traction. For investors, the current environment offers selective entry points, especially in logistics, life‑science labs, and adaptive‑reuse projects.

## Market Snapshot (2024‑2025 YTD)
| Sector | 2024 Avg. Cap Rate | 2025 YTD Avg. Cap Rate | YoY Change |
|--------|-------------------|------------------------|------------|
| Office (Class A) | 6.8% | 7.2% | +0.4 pts |
| Industrial (Warehouse) | 5.9% | 5.6% | -0.3 pts |
| Multi‑Family (Urban) | 5.4% | 5.5% | +0.1 pts |
| Retail (Neighborhood) | 6.5% | 6.8% | +0.3 pts |

> *Data sources: CoStar, JLL Q3 2024 CRE Report, Cook County Assessor.*

## Key Drivers
1. **E‑Commerce Logistics** – 33% of new warehouse space in 2024 was built to serve last‑mile distribution for Chicago‑area e‑commerce.
2. **Life‑Science Expansion** – The West Loop and Near‑North Side attract biotech firms; lab‑spec build‑to‑suit is up 12% YoY.
3. **Adaptive‑Reuse** – Conversions of under‑utilized office floors to residential or mixed‑use have increased 18% YoY, driven by zoning incentives.
4. **Infrastructure Investment** – Federal funding for the I‑90 expansion and Chicago’s “Green Loop” streetscape project is boosting outlook for adjacent parcels.

## Investment Strategies
- **Core‑Plus Industrial** – Target Class B warehouses within 15 mi of O’Hare or Midway; cap rates are compressing, delivering 5.5%‑6% yields with upside from rent escalations.
- **Life‑Science Build‑to‑Suit** – Partner with developers to secure long‑term leases (10‑15 yr) at 7‑8% returns; risk is mitigated by tenant credit quality.
- **Office‑to‑Residential Conversions** – Identify buildings with > 6% vacancy and explore entitlement for residential units; upside can be 30‑40% per‑unit price appreciation.
- **Opportunity Zone Funds** – Several Cook County ZIP codes (60645, 60540) qualify; tax‑deferral incentives can enhance IRR by 2‑3%.

## Risks & Mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Office oversupply | Focus on assets with strong tenant credit and consider conversion potential. |
| Interest‑rate volatility | Lock in fixed‑rate financing; prioritize cash‑flow positive properties. |
| Regulatory hurdles (zoning) | Work with local municipalities early; leverage Cook County’s adaptive‑reuse tax abatement programs. |

## Outlook
- **Industrial**: Cap rates likely to drift down 0.2‑0.3 points by end‑2025 as demand outpaces supply.
- **Office**: Stabilization expected in 2026 as hybrid work settles; investors should be selective.
- **Mixed‑Use**: High upside; expect increased developer activity in the Near‑South Side and West Loop.

## Conclusion
Cook County’s CRE market offers a blend of stable income and growth potential. By targeting logistics, life‑science, and adaptive‑reuse opportunities, investors can build diversified portfolios that hedge against sector‑specific downturns while capturing upside from the region’s economic resilience.
