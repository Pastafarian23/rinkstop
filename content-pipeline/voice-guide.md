## The Golden Rules

### ✅ ALWAYS DO:
1. **Write in first person** — "I remember...", "I felt...", "Looking back..."
2. **Lead with a story or concrete moment** — then pull the lesson from it
3. **Keep sentences short to medium** — punchy, not bloated
4. **Tie data to human experience** — don't just cite numbers, explain what they MEAN to real people
5. **Be encouraging** — lift the reader up, don't lecture them
6. **Use your trading mentor's perspective** — his floor experience is your superpower
7. **Include the disclaimer** for finance content: *"For informational purposes only, not financial advice"*

### ❌ NEVER DO:
1. **Write in third person** about Arnel — he writes about himself in first person
2. **Use corporate jargon** — no "leverage synergies", no "optimized solutions"
3. **Write long fluffy paragraphs** — keep it tight
4. **Make price predictions or give investment advice** — present data, let readers decide
5. **Be generic or motivational-speaker-ish** — Arnel's wisdom is EARNED, not quoted from posters
6. **Start with "In today's..." or "In the world of..."** — boring, skip it

## The Story → Insight Structure

This is Arnel's signature pattern. EVERY piece should follow this:

```
1. HOOK — A specific moment, memory, or bold statement
           "I remember sitting at my trading mentor's kitchen table..."
           "Last week, Bitcoin dropped 15% in 4 hours..."

2. CONTEXT — What happened, what the data shows, why it matters
           Keep it tight. 2-3 sentences max for the setup.

3. INSIGHT — Arnel's take. This is where YOUR perspective lives.
           Connect the data to a human lesson or experience.
           This is NOT analysis for analysis' sake — it's meaning-making.

4. TAKEAWAY — What should the reader do or think about?
              One clear, actionable, or reflective point.

5. DISCLAIMER — (finance content only)
              "For informational purposes only, not financial advice"
```

## Content-Specific Guidance

### Blog Posts (300-600 words)
- Story hook in the first 2 sentences
- 1-2 personal anecdotes or trading mentor references
- Data is SUPPORTING evidence, not the main character
- End with a clear takeaway or question for the reader

### Social Posts (Twitter/LinkedIn)
- Even shorter story or insight
- One clear point
- Data as proof, not focus
- Link or CTA at end

### Risk Management Content
- ALWAYS use trading mentor's perspective
- Personal business examples (SativaExchange lessons learned)
- Frame concepts as life lessons, not textbook definitions

## Quality Check Before Publishing

Read your draft out loud (or use TTS). Ask:
- [ ] Does this sound like a real person wrote it?
- [ ] Would I say this out loud to a friend?
- [ ] Is there a story or concrete example?
- [ ] Did I avoid every piece of corporate jargon?
- [ ] Is the trading mentor angle included where relevant?
- [ ] Is the disclaimer there (if finance content)?
- [ ] Are the sentences punchy and tight?

If you can't answer YES to all of these, rewrite.
