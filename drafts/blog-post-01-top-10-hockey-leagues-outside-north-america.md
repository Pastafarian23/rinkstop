**Body:**

When fans think hockey, they usually think NHL. But the professional game runs deep in Europe, Russia, and beyond. Here's RinkStop's ranking of the top 10 leagues outside North America — by talent level, international reach, and influence on the sport.

**1. KHL — Kontinental Hockey League**
*Russia and across Europe | Founded 2008 | 22 teams*

The KHL is the world's second-highest rated hockey league behind the NHL. It draws talent from Russia, Scandinavia, and beyond — and its playoffs draw massive viewership across 10+ countries. CSKA Moscow, SKA Saint Petersburg, and Jokerit Helsinki are among the powerhouse clubs.

**2. Swedish Hockey League (SHL)**
*Sweden | Founded 2013 (predecessors since 1975) | 14 teams*

Sweden's top league is a talent factory. The SHL consistently produces more NHL draft picks per capita than any league in the world. Frolunda Indians, HV71, and Brynas are perennial contenders. The pace and precision of Swedish hockey is unmatched at the club level.

**3. Liiga (Finnish Liiga)**
*Finland | Founded 1973 | 15 teams*

Finland's top league is small in scale but enormous in skill. The Liiga is widely considered one of the best-kept secrets in hockey — fast, smart, physical. Ilves Tampere and Tappara are the most decorated clubs. Finland's national team success tracks directly with Liiga's development record.

**4. Swiss National League**
*Switzerland | Founded 1933 | 13 teams*

One of Europe's oldest professional leagues, National League A combines Swiss precision with high-end import talent. HC Davos and ZSC Lions are internationally known brands. Switzerland's domestic league ranks third in European club coefficients.

**5. Czech Extraliga**
*Czech Republic | Founded 1993 | 14 teams*

The Czech Republic's top league is a direct descendant of the Czechoslovak league that produced some of the greatest players in hockey history. Sparta Prague and HC Olomouc are the flagship clubs. The Extraliga is known for physical play and strong goaltending.

**6. German DEL**
*Germany | Founded 1994 | 15 teams*

The DEL has grown into one of Europe's most-watched leagues. Adler Mannheim and EHC Red Bull Munchen have dominated recent seasons. The league attracts a mix of German stars and North American imports, and game atmosphere — especially in playoff hockey — rivals anywhere in the world.

**7. Slovak Extraliga**
*Slovakia | Founded 1993 | 11 teams*

Smaller than its neighbors, but Slovakia's top league punches above its weight. HK Duk Trencin and HC Kosice are the marquee clubs. The league is a key development ground for Slovak national team players who go on to the NHL and KHL.

**8. Norwegian GET-ligaen**
*Norway | Founded 1990 | 10 teams*

Norway's top league is growing fast — both in talent and infrastructure. Vålerenga Oslo is the country's flagship club with deep roots in Norwegian sports culture. The league has invested heavily in facilities over the past decade, and the national team's rise reflects that progress.
